Case study — Water Pavilion

A roof that floats,
a floor that reflects.

Lake Como, Italy. A cultural pavilion for a private foundation, open to the public for six months of the year. The brief asked for a room for concerts, readings and silence — and a building that would not compete with the lake.

The challenge

The site sits on a narrow strip between a protected shoreline and a working road. Construction access was limited to a single six-metre gate, and nothing could be built in the lake itself.

The move

We lifted the main volume onto four stone piers, allowing the lake to be seen beneath the building and the ground plane to remain public. The roof cantilevers eight metres, held by a post-tensioned stone beam.

The result

A column-free room for 180 people, naturally lit from above, with a shallow reflecting pool that cools incoming air. The pavilion operates off-grid for water and harvests rainwater from the roof.

Case study — Forest Retreat

Three volumes,
one slope.

A family house in the Swiss Alps, designed for two generations to share without living in each other's pockets. Three timber volumes step down the slope, connected by a glazed spine that becomes the life of the house in winter.

Client
Private
Scope
Architecture, interiors
Area
420 m²
Energy
Ground-source heat pump
Status
Completed 2023

The upper volume holds the living spaces and a long terrace facing the valley. The middle volume contains the bedrooms, set back behind a timber screen for privacy. The lowest volume is a self-contained apartment for visiting family, with its own entrance and kitchen.

All three volumes share the same spruce structure, exposed internally and charred externally. The roof is planted with local sedum, and the house draws all its heat from a ground-source loop buried beneath the meadow.
